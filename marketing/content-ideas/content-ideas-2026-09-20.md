# Everyday Adventures — Social Media Content Ideas
**Date:** 2026-09-20

---

## Idea 1
**Content Type:** Monday (Founder story / pain point)
**Pack Focus:** Bath 🛁
**Format:** Static image — dad sitting at a kitchen table at night, pen in hand, notebook open, warm lamp light

**Hook Line:**
> "After a bath time argument that ended in yelling and tears, my wife said: You need to think about how you want the girls to remember you. I started writing that night."

**Visual Direction:**
Fully clothed dad at a kitchen table, late at night. Warm, quiet lighting. Notebook and pen. No children. The mood is honest — not staged, not polished.

**Caption:**
That was 2001. I wrote activities for bath time, bedtime, mornings — anything that felt like a battle. I wasn't a parenting expert. I was just a dad who wanted to do better. Those notes sat in a box for 20 years. Now they're an app. 25 years later, it still works.

## Idea 2
**Content Type:** Wednesday (Activity demo / reveal)
**Pack Focus:** Bath 🛁
**Format:** Reel — quick cut demo showing the Robot Bath activity

**Hook Line:**
> "Bath time doesn't have to be a fight. Proof:"

**Visual Direction:**
Dad in full robot costume — cardboard box helmet, silver paint, the works — fully clothed, gesturing dramatically at a bathtub (child's perspective implied but no child shown). Over-the-top robot sounds. Playful and ridiculous. End card shows the app screen with the activity name.

**Caption:**
Robot Bath. It's exactly what it sounds like. Dad becomes a malfunctioning robot who can only clean ears when properly recharged. Kids don't even realize they're getting washed. This is one of 50+ activities in the Bath Pack. Chaos by design.

## Idea 4
**Content Type:** Monday (Founder story / pain point)
**Pack Focus:** None — origin story
**Format:** Reel — talking head, dad to camera, no script feel

**Hook Line:**
> "I pitched this to Scholastic in 2004. They passed."

**Visual Direction:**
Dad talking directly to camera, casual setting — kitchen, living room. Real clothes. No teleprompter feel. Cut to a photo of an old box or notebook (optional). End on phone showing the app.

**Caption:**
Life happened. The box sat. My daughters grew up — they're 30 and 26 now. Then in 2026 I finally built the app. Here's the wild part: every single activity still works exactly the same. Turns out toddlers haven't changed much in 25 years.

## Idea 10
**Content Type:** Monday (Founder story)
**Pack Focus:** None — full origin arc
**Format:** Carousel — 6 slides telling the 25-year story in short punchy text

**Hook Line:**
> "25 years ago I wrote activities to be a better dad. Here's what happened."

**Visual Direction:**
Each slide: single sentence, warm background color (rotate between soft yellows, blues, creams). Year markers as context. No people. Final slide: app icon + download prompt. Feels like a mini-documentary in card form.

**Slide Sequence:**
1. "2001. Bath time ended in yelling and tears."
2. "My wife said: think about how you want the girls to remember you."
3. "I started writing that night."
4. "2004. Pitched it to Scholastic. They passed."
5. "Life happened. The box sat for 20 years."
6. "2026. It's finally an app. My daughters are 30 and 26. The activities still work."

**Caption:**
After a bath time argument that ended in yelling and tears, my wife said: You need to think about how you want the girls to remember you. I started writing that night. 25 years later, it's an app. Some things are worth waiting for.
