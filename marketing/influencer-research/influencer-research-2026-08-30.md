## 2. Communities & Groups to Engage In

### 1. r/Parenting (Reddit)
- **URL:** https://www.reddit.com/r/Parenting/
- **Size/Activity:** 8M+ members; dozens of new posts per hour, 24/7 activity. ~20% of posts are tagged "Toddler 1–3 years."
- **How to add value:** Lurk for 2–4 weeks before posting. Participate as a parent and toddler activity expert — answer questions like "what do you do on a rainy day with a 3-year-old?" from genuine experience. When the app is directly the answer to a question, mention it briefly, disclose your role, and link to a free resource rather than a sales page.

### 2. r/toddlers (Reddit)
- **URL:** https://www.reddit.com/r/toddlers/
- **Size/Activity:** Large, active community purpose-built for the 1–4 age range. Covers tantrums, sleep, speech, activities, and behavior — tightly focused.
- **How to add value:** Build a posting history answering specific questions about toddler activities, development milestones, and routines over several weeks. This community's specificity makes it easier to add genuine value before any brand mention.

### 3. r/Daddit (Reddit)
- **URL:** https://www.reddit.com/r/Daddit/
- **Size/Activity:** Large, well-established; one of Reddit's most wholesome subreddits, with a reputation for genuine mutual support among dads.
- **How to add value:** Josh's identity as the dad founder is a natural fit here. Participate in weekly pinned threads. Share real parenting experiences. When the moment is right, the founder story ("I made activities for my daughters 22 years ago and finally built the app") lands as authentic community contribution, not self-promotion.

### 4. BabyCenter Community
- **URL:** https://www.babycenter.com/community
- **Size/Activity:** 3.7M+ monthly unique visitors (UK alone; US is larger). Birth clubs for 2022–2024 babies are currently in toddler territory — high relevance right now.
- **How to add value:** BabyCenter has strict anti-spam rules, so direct promotion will get posts removed. The right move is to participate as an expert voice on toddler activities and routines over weeks. Also consider reaching out to BabyCenter's editorial team for a content partnership or expert Q&A — they regularly feature founder voices.

### 5. Facebook Niche Toddler Activity Groups
- **Examples:** Search Facebook for "toddler activities at home," "outdoor toddler activities," "sensory play for toddlers"
- **Size/Activity:** Individual niche groups range from 10K–130K+ members; the best ones post multiple times daily.
- **How to add value:** Join 3–5 hyper-relevant groups and read rules carefully before posting anything. Spend 2–4 weeks answering questions and contributing value. Offer a free resource (a tip sheet, a sample activity PDF) where rules allow. Never post promotional content without admin permission.

## 3. Podcasts & Blogs Worth Pitching

- **URL:** https://www.startupdadpod.com | Contact: https://www.startupdadpod.com/contact/
- **Host:** Adam Fishman | Instagram DM: @startupdadpod
- **Audience focus:** Tech executives, entrepreneurs, and company founders who are also fathers. Webby Award-winning. Covers the intersection of building something and raising kids.
- **Why the founder story resonates:** This is the show's exact wheelhouse. A dad who invented toddler activities for his daughters in 2004, got rejected by Scholastic, and spent 22 years before launching as an app in 2026 is a textbook Startup Dad arc — personal motivation, commercial rejection, long perseverance, and a product born directly from fatherhood. ## 5. One Opportunity That Surprised Me

**The Startup Dad Podcast is an almost implausibly perfect fit — and it's pitchable right now.**

Going in, I expected the best podcast targets to be general parenting shows. What I found instead was a Webby Award-winning show built entirely around the intersection of founding a company and fatherhood — and Josh's story hits every single note it's designed for: a product born from his daughters, a major publisher rejection, a 22-year arc from idea to launch.

What makes this particularly high-potential: most Startup Dad guests are current startup founders pitching a business narrative. Josh's story is different — it's a *slow* story, a patience story, a "I didn't build this to get rich, I built this for my kids" story. That emotional texture stands out in a genre that can skew toward hustle-culture. Adam Fishman specifically seeks the human story behind the product. The pitch writes itself: "I invented these activities for my daughters in 2004. Scholastic passed. I finally launched the app 22 years later." That's a compelling radio/podcast moment before a single feature is mentioned.

Pitch it first. The others can follow.
