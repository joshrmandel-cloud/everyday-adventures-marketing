## 1. Influencers & Creators to Check Out

### 1. Busy Toddler — @busytoddler
- **Platform:** Instagram (~2.4M followers), blog at busytoddler.com
- **Why relevant:** Susie Allison is a former kindergarten teacher with a Master's in Early Childhood Education. Her entire brand is simple, play-based activities for toddlers — bath time, sensory play, learning through everyday moments. She explicitly covers morning and bedtime transitions. Her audience is exactly our target parent.
- **Suggested approach:** Engage genuinely with her content for 2–3 weeks (comment on activity posts with specific, helpful additions). Then DM or use the contact form at busytoddler.com to propose a collaboration — maybe framing Josh's bath activities as something her audience hasn't seen before. She likely gets many cold pitches, so warm engagement first matters.

### 2. Big Little Feelings — @biglittlefeelings
- **Platform:** Instagram (~3.5M followers), podcast "After Bedtime with Big Little Feelings" (relaunched June 2025)
- **Why relevant:** Run by licensed child therapist Deena Margolin and parent coach Kristin Gallant, named in Time's 100 Most Influential Creators list (July 2026, Catalyst category). They focus on toddler emotional regulation — tantrums, bedtime resistance, transitions. Their audience is deeply invested in making daily routines smoother, which is exactly what Everyday Adventures delivers.
- **Suggested approach:** Their podcast "After Bedtime" is a direct pitch opportunity. Josh's story (activities developed for his own daughters, rejected by Scholastic, finished 22 years later) fits their storytelling style. Follow the account, engage with posts, then pitch to the podcast via their website. Also worth checking if they accept sponsored content for app reviews.

### 3. TheDadLab — @thedadlab (Sergei Urban)
- **Platform:** YouTube (5M+ subscribers), Instagram (~1.2M followers)
- **Why relevant:** The most prominent dad-focused educational content creator in the space. His channel is built around a dad doing hands-on activities with his kids — exactly Josh's origin story. His audience already self-selects for "dads who do activities with young children." Everyday Adventures is a natural fit for a video like "5 bath time games my toddler loves."
- **Suggested approach:** DM via Instagram or reach out through his website/contact page. A collaboration pitch could frame Josh as a fellow dad-creator with a 20-year backstory. If he doesn't collaborate, leaving thoughtful comments on his videos can still drive visibility.

### 4. Raising Boys & Girls — @raisingboysandgirls
- **Platform:** Podcast + Instagram + blog at raisingboysandgirls.com
- **Why relevant:** This show has featured the founder of 1000 Hours Outside — a parent who built an outdoor movement from a personal practice with their kids. That precedent is almost identical to Josh's founder story. They cover daily routines, outdoor play, and child development for the same target age range.
- **Suggested approach:** Pitch Josh as a podcast guest (see Section 3 for details). Also follow their Instagram and engage in comments to build familiarity before a cold pitch lands.

### 5. Darien Rencher — @darienrencher
- **Platform:** Instagram (~268K followers)
- **Why relevant:** A mid-tier creator documenting toddler life and preparing for baby #2 — exactly the growth phase where a parent most wants new activity ideas. His more modest following means he's more accessible to collaborate with than mega-influencers, and his audience is highly engaged and in the target age window.
- **Suggested approach:** Follow and comment genuinely. Consider gifting app access and asking if he'd share his honest experience. Mid-tier creators often have better engagement rates than accounts with millions of followers.

## 2. Communities & Groups to Engage In

### 1. r/toddlers
- **URL:** https://www.reddit.com/r/toddlers/
- **Size:** ~548K members, growing (stats updated October 12, 2026 per GummySearch)
- **How to add value:** This community asks extremely specific questions — "how do I make bath time less of a battle," "what do I do when my 3-year-old won't go to bed," "rainy day and losing my mind." Josh can answer these questions from lived experience, sharing what worked with his own daughters, without promoting the app directly. Once he's a recognized voice in the community, sharing the app as a resource feels organic, not spammy. Start by answering 10 questions before mentioning the app once.

### 2. r/Parenting
- **URL:** https://www.reddit.com/r/Parenting/
- **Size:** ~8.3M members (the largest general parenting community on Reddit)
- **How to add value:** Large but broad. Best approach is to search for threads about toddler routines, bath time struggles, or rainy day activities and contribute thoughtful answers. Avoid promotional language. The sheer size means even a single well-received comment can drive meaningful traffic. Josh's authentic "dad who figured this out the hard way" voice will stand out.

### 3. r/Preschoolers
- **URL:** https://www.reddit.com/r/Preschoolers/
- **Size:** Smaller than r/toddlers but tightly focused on ages 3–5, which overlaps directly with Everyday Adventures' target age range
- **How to add value:** Great place to share specific activity ideas (Good Mornings pack, Rainy Days pack) in the context of genuine conversation. Parents here are asking about specific developmental moments — a comment like "what my daughter and I used to do at this age was..." resonates in this more intimate community.

### 4. BabyCenter Community Forums
- **URL:** https://community.babycenter.com/ (accessible via babycenter.com)
- **Size:** One of the largest parenting forums on the web, with boards organized by child's age and topic
- **How to add value:** Boards like "Toddler Behavior & Development" and "Activities for Toddlers" see consistent activity. Josh can post under his real name and mention his background as a dad who developed these activities — the BabyCenter audience responds well to authentic parent voices vs. polished brands.

### 5. Facebook: "Overstimulated Moms Club"
- **URL:** Search for "Overstimulated Moms Club" on Facebook
- **Size:** 370,000+ members, predominantly English-speaking parents covering toddler and family topics
- **How to add value:** The "overstimulated" framing is the exact moment Everyday Adventures solves — what do I do with this kid right now. Josh (or whoever manages the page) can share free activity ideas when parents post "I'm out of ideas, help," and build credibility before mentioning the app.

## 3. Podcasts & Blogs Worth Pitching

### 1. One Bad Mother (Maximum Fun)
- **URL:** https://maximumfun.org/podcasts/one-bad-mother/
- **Audience:** Parents of young children, skewing toward moms; focuses on the messy, real, imperfect side of parenting. Known for humor and authenticity.
- **Why Josh's story resonates:** A dad who developed activities for his daughters in 2004, got rejected by Scholastic, and finally shipped it 22 years later is exactly the kind of "I didn't give up on this weird thing I cared about" story this audience loves. The Scholastic rejection adds stakes and relatability.
- **How to pitch:** Email **onebadmother@maximumfun.org** — the show's own episode pages list this as the address for listener topic suggestions and guest pitches. Keep it short: one paragraph on who Josh is, one sentence on the Scholastic rejection, one sentence on the app. Offer to come on to talk about how he developed the activities with his girls.

### 2. Transforming the Toddler Years (Cara Tyrrell)
- **URL:** https://www.caratyrrell.com/podcast
- **Audience:** Parents of toddlers focused on behavior, connection, and calm routines. The show features expert guests on practical toddler challenges.
- **Why Josh's story resonates:** The show is explicitly about transforming toddler routines — bath, bedtime, morning. Josh's 5 packs map directly onto its episode themes. The founder-as-practitioner angle (dad of daughters, not a researcher) offers a different voice than the show's typical expert guests, which could make him stand out in a pitch.
- **How to pitch:** Contact via caratyrrell.com. Mention the specific packs (Bath, Good Nights, Good Mornings) and frame the pitch around the idea that these activities were developed by a parent in the trenches, not sold down from a development expert.

### 3. Raising Boys & Girls Podcast
- **URL:** https://www.raisingboysandgirls.com/podcast
- **Audience:** Parents of kids roughly ages 2–12, focused on character development, outdoor play, and everyday family rituals. They have featured the founder of 1000 Hours Outside — a parent who built a movement from a personal practice.
- **Why Josh's story resonates:** The 1000 Hours Outside precedent is the clearest possible signal. A dad who spent years developing activities for his daughters, got rejected by a big publisher, and kept going is exactly the kind of founder story this show has already demonstrated it loves. The app's focus on small, daily moments (bath, bedtime, morning) aligns with their values around intentional family time.
- **How to pitch:** Use the contact page at raisingboysandgirls.com. Reference the 1000 Hours Outside episode specifically as an example of the kind of story you're bringing — it shows you know the show and aren't blasting a generic pitch.

## 5. One Opportunity You Found That Surprised Me

**Big Little Feelings was just named to Time's 100 Most Influential Creators (July 2026)** — in the "Catalyst" category, which Time defines as creators who are actively changing something. The Axios coverage specifically credits them with helping mainstream gentle parenting and making therapist-informed advice accessible at scale.

What makes this surprising and useful for Everyday Adventures: their core message is that toddler behavior problems (tantrums, bedtime resistance, bath refusal) are communication problems that respond to *activities and engagement*, not discipline. That framing makes their 3.5M-follower audience a near-perfect match for an app full of activities designed to make those exact moments easier.

They also just relaunched their own podcast, "After Bedtime with Big Little Feelings," in June 2025 — meaning they are actively building a new content surface and may be looking for fresh founder stories with strong resonance for their audience.

The opportunity: pitch Josh's story specifically to the "After Bedtime" podcast, framing it as a dad who solved the same problems their audience faces, 20 years before anyone was talking about gentle parenting. That framing is both timely (it fits their brand moment) and emotionally compelling.
