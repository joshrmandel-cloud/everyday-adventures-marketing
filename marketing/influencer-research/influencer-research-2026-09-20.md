## 5. One Opportunity That Surprised Me

**The Screen Anxiety Counter-Narrative Is Peaking Right Now**

A TechCrunch article from May 2026 covered Maka Kids, a children's streaming app explicitly designed around toddler well-being rather than engagement. The article signals that the mainstream parenting press is actively looking for alternatives to addictive-by-design children's apps — and the Everyday Adventures positioning (activities invented by a real dad, low-screen, routine-based) fits this counter-narrative perfectly.

Simultaneously, Pinterest's 2026 parenting trend report flagged a surge in parent searches for "routine charts," "at-home learning support," and "cognitive worksheets for toddlers" — all driven by parental anxiety about screen time.

**The opportunity:** Josh should explicitly own the "I built these before smartphones existed" angle. The 2004 origin story isn't just charming — it's a credibility signal that this content was designed for real human connection, not for a screen. Pitching to tech-and-parenting press (like TechCrunch's parenting beat, or Mack Rack-connected journalists who covered Maka Kids) with that framing could generate earned media that no influencer campaign can match.

**Suggested action:** Draft a 200-word founder story blurb with the explicit framing "designed in 2004 — before anyone had a smartphone — and still working in 2026" and have it ready for any press outreach this fall.
