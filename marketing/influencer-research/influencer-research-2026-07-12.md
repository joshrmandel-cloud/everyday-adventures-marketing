## 2. Communities & Groups to Engage In

### 1. r/toddlers — reddit.com/r/toddlers
- **Size:** ~380,000 subscribers
- **Activity:** Consistently active; daily posts on tantrums, sleep regressions, potty training, keeping toddlers busy, language development, and — critically — bedtime and bath routines.
- **How Josh can add value:** Participate as a parent first. Share one specific activity from the app in a "what worked for us tonight at bath time" format post — no selling, just sharing. Once trusted in the community, a "I built an app based on activities I developed for my own kids — here are a few free ones" post would be well received. Read the community rules before posting any app mention.

---

### 2. r/Daddit — reddit.com/r/daddit
- **Size:** 1.9M members
- **Activity:** Very active; one of the largest dad-only communities on Reddit. Common threads include advice requests, book/product recommendations (bedtime books come up frequently), and humor about toddler chaos.
- **How Josh can add value:** This is the single highest-alignment community for Josh's persona — a real dad who built something for his kids. His story (activities he created in 2004 for his daughters, finally finished the app at 46) would land well as an AMA ("I'm a dad who developed toddler activities in 2004 and finally shipped the app — AMA"). This should be approached thoughtfully and only after establishing some presence in comments first.

---

### 3. r/Parenting — reddit.com/r/Parenting
- **Size:** 5M+ members
- **Activity:** The largest parenting community on Reddit; broad coverage of all ages and stages.
- **How Josh can add value:** Best used to answer specific questions about toddler bedtime resistance, bath time struggles, or rainy day boredom. Being a helpful commenter with real, specific suggestions (that happen to come from the same methodology as the app) builds credibility without direct promotion. Reference the app only when directly relevant and the community rules permit it.

---

### 4. r/sciencebasedparenting — reddit.com/r/sciencebasedparenting
- **Size:** Smaller but highly engaged; research-oriented parents
- **Activity:** Members discuss studies, expert consensus, and evidence-based approaches to parenting decisions.
- **How Josh can add value:** Share the developmental thinking behind Everyday Adventures' structured activities. A post like "Here's why we built simple, repetitive bath-time activities for toddlers — and what the research says about predictable routines" would be welcomed here. This audience is skeptical of marketing but receptive to substance.

### 5. Facebook Group: "Toddler Activities & Play Ideas" (and similar large groups)
- **Size:** Groups in this niche commonly have 200K–500K+ members
- **Activity:** Facebook remains active for this demographic (parents 28–38); toddler activity groups see high daily engagement with photos and ideas.
- **How Josh can add value:** Post a free activity ("Here's what we do on rainy days when screen time runs out") as a genuine contribution. Photo-forward posts with simple setups perform well. Avoid any direct app promotion in initial posts — build trust first, then offer the app as a resource in comments when others ask "where can I find more like this?"

## 5. One Opportunity That Surprised Me

**Google Play's #WeArePlay program** is a high-visibility, free opportunity that almost no one thinks to pursue.

Google runs an ongoing series profiling the real people behind apps on the Play Store — their origin stories, their motivations, what they built and why. Past featured stories have included solo developers, parent-founders, and people who built apps for personal reasons before ever thinking about a business. Google's Android Developers Blog published 153 new #WeArePlay US stories in one batch — the program is active and actively looking for submissions.

Josh's story hits every note Google spotlights: a real person, a personal origin (his daughters), a long journey (22 years from idea to ship), and a product with clear positive impact for families. This isn't a media pitch — it's a platform nomination form through the Google Play Console — and a feature would put Everyday Adventures in front of millions of Android users alongside editorial credibility from Google itself.

**What to do:** Visit play.google.com/console/about/weareplay-us/ and submit Josh's story. The nomination process asks for the founder story and app impact — both of which Everyday Adventures has in abundance. This is worth prioritizing in the next two weeks while the app is freshly launched.
