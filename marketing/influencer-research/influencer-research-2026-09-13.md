## 1. Influencers & Creators to Check Out

### 1. Susie Allison — @busytoddler
- **Platform:** Instagram (2.4M+ followers), busytoddler.com
- **Why relevant:** Former kindergarten teacher with a Master's in Early Childhood Education. Her entire brand is built on simple, play-based activities for toddlers that parents can do at home — daily routines, independent play, and making ordinary moments count. This is the exact philosophy behind Everyday Adventures: bath time, bedtime, and rainy days are her bread and butter.
- **Suggested approach:** Start by following and genuinely engaging with 3–5 posts. Then DM with a personal note from Josh — lead with the "built these for my own daughters in 2004" angle. Offer a free app trial, not a paid deal. Her audience trusts her because she only promotes things she actually believes in.

### 2. Sergei Urban — @thedadlab
- **Platform:** Instagram (1.1M+), YouTube (5M+ subscribers)

His audience is dads actively looking for things to do with young kids.

### 4. Dr. Becky Kennedy — @drbeckyatgoodinside

### 5. Janet Lansbury — @janetlansbury

## 5. One Opportunity You Found That Surprised You

**TheDadLab's trajectory is a near-perfect roadmap for Everyday Adventures — and a potential direct outreach opportunity.**

Sergei Urban started exactly like Josh: a dad doing activities with his young sons at home. He had no publishing deal, no corporate backing. He started sharing content online, and it grew to 10M+ followers, a best-selling book, and his own app.

The surprise isn't just that the stories rhyme — it's that Sergei now *speaks at baby and parenting expos* (he's confirmed at The Baby Show 2026 in the UK). These expos — The Baby Show, the National Parenting Products Awards, What to Expect events — are not marketing channels most indie app founders think about. But they're exactly where the target audience of parents with 0–4 year olds concentrates in one room, in buying mode.

**The opportunity:** Sergei's path suggests Josh should look at pitching parenting expos and family conferences as a speaker, not just an exhibitor. The "dad who built this for his daughters 20 years ago" story is expo gold — it gives organizers a narrative that's better than a brand demo. And speaking slots bring credibility that ads can't buy.

Reach out to Sergei directly as a peer, and look at the US equivalents of The Baby Show (bump, Baby & Beyond; What to Expect events; BabyFair). The founder story is the pitch.
