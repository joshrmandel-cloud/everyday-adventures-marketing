## 1. Influencers & Creators to Check Out

### 1. Susie Allison — @busytoddler
- **Platform:** Instagram (primary), YouTube
- **Followers:** ~2.4 million on Instagram (DolphinRadar, Aug 2026)
- **Why relevant:** Susie is a former kindergarten and first-grade teacher with an M.Ed. in Early Childhood Education who built her following entirely around screen-free toddler play. Her content is almost entirely activity-based for ages 2–5, and her audience is exactly the 28–38 parent demo we want. Bath activities, morning play, and rainy day setups are her bread and butter. Everyday Adventures' packs map directly onto her content pillars.
- **Suggested approach:** Follow and genuinely engage with her activity posts (comment substantively, not generically). When you've built a few interactions, send a DM with the founder story — "a dad who created these for his daughters in 2004" is a story she would find compelling. Pitch a collaboration where she demos one of the Bath or Rainy Days pack activities.

### 2. Sergei Urban — @thedadlab
- **Platform:** YouTube (5.1M subscribers), Instagram
- **Why relevant:** The Dad Lab is the most prominent dad-focused kids-activity creator online. Sergei makes hands-on experiments and activities that dads and kids do together — a perfect fit for Josh's "dad who built these with his daughters" story. His audience skews toward dads actively engaged with their kids, which aligns well with the secondary-but-high-value dad segment.
- **Suggested approach:** Reach out via the contact link on his site (netinfluencer.com lists a contact route). Frame the pitch around dad-to-dad resonance: "I'm a dad who wrote these activities in 2004 for my daughters — here's what 25 years of refinement looks like." The science-experiment angle of Rainy Days could be a natural fit for a video.

### 3. Daisy Upton — @fiveminutemum
- **Platform:** Instagram (~270K followers), blog, books
- **Why relevant:** Daisy is a UK-based teaching assistant turned bestselling author who makes five-minute games and activities for toddlers at home. Her follower count is smaller but her audience is highly engaged and exactly on target — parents seeking quick, low-prep activities for young kids. "Five minutes" and "everyday adventures" share the same philosophy: short, high-value, no setup required.
- **Suggested approach:** Comment genuinely on several posts, then send a DM introducing the app and the founder story. Her UK base means she's unlikely to become an affiliate partner immediately, but a mention or story share would reach an aligned audience. Look for a "routine" hook — she posts regularly about morning and bedtime play.

### 4. Dr. Siggie Cohen — @dr.siggie
- **Platform:** Instagram
- **Why relevant:** Dr. Siggie is a child psychologist and parenting expert who posts extensively about toddler routines — specifically bath time, bedtime, and morning transitions. Her content gives scientific backing to why routines matter for kids ages 2–5. Everyday Adventures is essentially a tool that makes Dr. Siggie's advice actionable, which makes this a natural alignment.
- **Suggested approach:** Engage with her content by adding value in comments (reference specific packs when she posts about routines). Then pitch a collaboration framed as: "You explain the science of why routines matter for toddlers; we built the activities that make those routines fun." A joint Instagram Live or Story takeover could work well.

### 5. Jamie Reimer — @handsonaswegrowinsta (Hands On As We Grow)
- **Platform:** Blog (handsonaswegrowinsta.com), Instagram, Pinterest
- **Why relevant:** Hands On As We Grow is a well-established blog and social presence focused entirely on practical, low-prep activities for toddlers and preschoolers. Jamie posts activity ideas organized by theme and age — very similar to the Everyday Adventures pack structure. Her audience trusts her for ideas they can do today, with what they have at home.
- **Suggested approach:** Pitch a guest post or content collaboration. The founder story — dad develops activities for daughters in 2004, Scholastic passes, finishes it 25 years later — is exactly the kind of human-interest story parenting blogs love. Offer to write a post about the origin story with one or two activities from the app as samples.

## 2. Communities & Groups to Engage In

### 1. r/toddlers
- **URL:** https://www.reddit.com/r/toddlers
- **Size:** ~443,000–539,000 members (GummySearch, 2026); very high activity
- **How to add value:** This is the primary hub for parents of 2–4 year olds on Reddit. Josh could post under his own name, sharing one specific activity (e.g., a bath game that turns teeth-brushing into a mission) as a self-contained tip, not an ad. Lead with the activity, mention the app only at the end or in comments if asked. Posts framed as "What I do with my daughter during bath time" consistently perform well here. Avoid anything that reads like a launch announcement.

### 2. r/daddit
- **URL:** https://www.reddit.com/r/daddit
- **Size:** ~1.7–2.0 million members; massive, very active
- **How to add value:** r/daddit is one of the largest and warmest communities on Reddit. The founder story is perfect for this audience: a dad who spent 20 years refining play activities for his kids is exactly what this community celebrates. Josh could post the origin story authentically — "In 2004 I wrote down every game I played with my daughters during bath time. Scholastic passed. I finally turned it into an app 25 years later. Here's what I learned about toddler attention spans." That kind of vulnerable, specific, dad-to-dad narrative performs extremely well here.

### 3. r/Parenting
- **URL:** https://www.reddit.com/r/Parenting
- **Size:** 8M+ members (AAAI research, November 2025); the largest parenting community on Reddit
- **How to add value:** This broad community is harder to break through but has the highest ceiling. The best approach is to answer questions, not to promote. Whenever someone posts "What do you do during bath time to make it less chaotic?" or "Rainy day ideas for a 3-year-old?" — Josh can answer genuinely and helpfully, and mention the app only when directly relevant and asked.

### 4. Facebook: "Toddler Activities & Play Ideas" groups
- **Search approach:** Facebook's largest active toddler-activity groups include "Kids' Crafts and Activities" (73K members, per Tinybeans roundup) and general local parent groups organized by city. Search "toddler activities [city]" to find active local groups.
- **How to add value:** Post one activity from the app as a free tip (e.g., a Good Mornings game that works with what's already in the kitchen). Photos and short videos perform best. Never lead with "download my app" — lead with the activity, and let the app be the discovery.

### 5. Tinybeans Community / App
- **URL:** https://tinybeans.com
- **Size:** Tinybeans has millions of registered parent users and an active editorial community
- **How to add value:** Tinybeans is a private photo-sharing and milestone-tracking app for parents with young children. Their editorial team publishes parenting activity content. Pitch Tinybeans' editorial team to be featured in their "activities for toddlers" content, or explore whether they have a community forum or group feature for direct engagement. This is both a community play and a potential editorial/press opportunity.

## 3. Podcasts & Blogs Worth Pitching

### 1. The Teaching Your Toddler Podcast
- **URL:** https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/the-teaching-your-toddler-podcast/id1347577177 — also at teachingyourtoddlershow.libsyn.com
- **Host:** Marijo Tinlin
- **Audience:** Moms ages 25–45 who are home with their young children, looking for activities to do with them (SponsorPitch listing). This is the most direct audience match of any podcast found this week.
- **Why the founder story resonates:** The show features interview episodes focused on moms, parenting, and "fun activities to do with your kiddos." Josh's story — a dad who developed activities for his daughters in 2004, got a Scholastic meeting, was passed on, and finished it 25 years later — is exactly the kind of surprising, feel-good founder arc that resonates on shows like this.
- **How to pitch:** Email subscribe@teachingyourtoddler.com. Keep it short: one paragraph on the founder story, one sentence on the app, one line on why it fits her audience. Ask for a 20-minute interview episode slot.

### 2. Startup Dad (Adam Fishman)
- **URL:** https://startupdadpod.substack.com — Apple Podcasts: https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/id1693312339
- **Host:** Adam Fishman (product executive and Substack newsletter author)
- **Audience:** Tech-adjacent founders and executives who are also fathers. Recent guests include CEOs and co-founders of startups, all with a parenting angle.
- **Why the founder story resonates:** The show explicitly covers fatherhood + company-building. Josh's story is a perfect fit: a dad who built something for his kids in 2004, faced rejection, kept the idea alive for over two decades, and shipped it as an app in 2026. That's a compelling founder arc with a deeply personal family hook. The 25-year timeline from idea to launch is unusual enough to be memorable.
- **How to pitch:** Email podcast@fishmana.com (listed for sponsorship, but likely the general contact). Write a two-paragraph pitch: paragraph one is the founder story, paragraph two is how it connects to Adam's "fatherhood + building" theme.

### 3. Respectful Parenting: Janet Lansbury Unruffled
- **URL:** https://www.janetlansbury.com/podcast — also on Apple Podcasts
- **Host:** Janet Lansbury, internationally recognized parenting author and RIE practitioner
- **Audience:** Parents of infants and toddlers who are invested in intentional, respectful approaches to parenting. Large, loyal audience.
- **Why the founder story resonates:** Unruffled is built around the idea that small, intentional moments with young children matter enormously. Everyday Adventures embodies that philosophy — activities designed to make ordinary routines (bath, bedtime, tooth brushing) into moments of connection. The "dad who did this for his own daughters" angle is emotionally resonant for her audience. The show accepts guests and has featured educators, researchers, and practitioners.
- **How to pitch:** Check janetlansbury.com for a contact or guest form. Frame the pitch around the philosophy match, not the app: "I spent 20 years refining these activities with my daughters. I'd love to talk about how ordinary routines become extraordinary memories." Lead with the relational angle, not the product.

## 5. One Opportunity That Surprised Me

**Scholastic just launched a streaming app for kids — validating Josh's 2004 pitch 25 years too late.**

In September 2025, Scholastic announced its own streaming app for children ages 2–12, bringing 400+ hours of content from their library to digital screens. This is almost exactly what Josh pitched to Scholastic in 2004.

Why this matters for marketing: **This is the most compelling part of the founder story, and it's not being used yet.**

The pitch to Scholastic in 2004, their pass, and now their own belated entry into kids digital content is a perfect "ahead of his time" narrative. It is the kind of story that:
- Gets picked up by parenting blogs and tech press
- Makes for a compelling hook on podcasts like Startup Dad
- Creates genuine social media moments: "The company that passed on my pitch in 2004 just launched a version of what I built — 25 years later. My daughters were right."

This isn't just a fun aside — it's a PR angle. Pitch the founder story to parenting tech writers, dad-focused newsletters, and podcasts with the Scholastic detail front and center. It makes the 25-year arc feel validated rather than delayed.

**Suggested action:** Draft a short LinkedIn or social post this week using this framing. It will do better than any ad.
